## 1. Required handwritten sequence

The scanned pages should cover the following sequence in the student's own handwriting:

1. Define two uncalibrated projective cameras, homogeneous 3D points, and homogeneous image
   points.
2. Write the projection relationships for the two views and identify known, measured, and
   unknown quantities.
3. Define corresponding points and derive the epipolar relationship using the fundamental
   matrix.
4. Explain how image correspondences are used to estimate the fundamental matrix, including
   normalization, outlier handling, and the rank constraint at the level required by the course.
5. Establish the epipoles/epipolar lines and explain the geometric meaning of the constraint.
6. Show a projective camera pair and the triangulation/reconstruction relationship.
7. Relate reconstructed 3D point distances to object edge lengths and dimensions.
8. Explain projective ambiguity and the separate metric-scale ambiguity.
9. State what additional metric information or assumptions are needed for absolute dimensions,
   such as a known reference length, calibrated intrinsics/baseline, known camera motion, or
   enough constraints for a metric upgrade.
10. Complete every assigned numerical example and intermediate calculation by hand.

The final pages must distinguish theoretical derivation from any real experiment. No fabricated
correspondences, dimensions, or numerical results may be introduced.
